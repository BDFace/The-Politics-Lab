Women got the board seats. They still don’t get the top job

The data behind the story, and how we checked it

All the European comparisons in the story come from one public source: the European Institute for Gender Equality’s count of women and men in the largest listed companies of each European country. We worked out every share again from the raw headcounts, and checked each number twice: once in a spreadsheet and once in a separate program written in R.

What the words mean

In the storyWhat EIGE calls itWho it covers
Board seatsNon-executive board membersPeople who oversee the company but don’t run it day to day. In countries with two boards, the supervisory board.
Top managementSenior executivesMembers of the company’s two highest decision-making bodies who run it day to day.
Chief executiveCEOThe one person in charge of running the company. One per company, so EIGE’s count of chief executives is also its count of companies.

How we checked it

Shares from headcounts. EIGE publishes both headcounts and percentages. We used the headcounts and divided women by the total ourselves. Our shares match EIGE’s own percentages to within 0.05 points.

Groups pooled by people, not averaged. “Quota countries” are Norway, Iceland, France, Italy, Belgium, Germany, Austria and Portugal, which had binding laws with sanctions before 2020. “The rest of Europe” is the other 21 EU countries. For each group we added up all the women and all the people, rather than averaging the countries’ percentages, so a country with more companies counts for more.

The comparison period. The second half of 2024, the last period with figures for the UK.

Other ways of drawing the groups. Counting the Netherlands and Greece as quota countries gives top management shares of 24.8% against 23.3%. Comparing only with western and northern Europe gives 25.3% against 24.1%. The result, a gap of about one to two points, holds either way.

Figures from UK reports. The FTSE 100 and FTSE 350 figures come from the Davies Review (February 2011) and the FTSE Women Leaders Review (February 2026), not from EIGE. The check workbook lists each one with its page number.
