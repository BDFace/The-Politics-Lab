About The Coffeehouse Lab

Independent, data-led analysis that shows what the evidence says about British politics and policy, and what it means for decisions.

Each story starts with a question the data can answer, and ends with what the answer means for policy, and what the data cannot tell us. We compare Britain with other countries when that helps.

The name comes from the coffeehouses of 18th-century London, where people read the news together and argued about it.

Independence and funding

The Coffeehouse Lab is self-funded. It has no funders, advertisers or political affiliation. If that changes, this page will say so.

We don’t endorse parties or candidates, and we never tell readers how to vote. We pick the question before we know which side the answer helps, and publish the answer either way.

How we check the numbers

  • Official statistics come first, then peer-reviewed research, then reputable research institutes.
  • Every number in a story, its charts and its social posts is listed in a check workbook, with a formula that works it out from the raw data.
  • A separate program written in R downloads the raw data again and recomputes the headline numbers, so the two checks can disagree.
  • Each story’s data page lets you download the files and see the definitions, the method and the limits.
  • We say “linked to” unless the evidence supports “caused”, and every story says what its data cannot show.

Corrections

If we get something wrong, we fix it and add a dated note at the foot of the story saying what changed and why. Every correction is listed on the corrections page.

Photos

We use photos under free licences that allow commercial use, and credit each one. We never use AI-generated images of real people or realistic news scenes. Charts are drawn for The Coffeehouse Lab from the data listed on each story’s data page.
